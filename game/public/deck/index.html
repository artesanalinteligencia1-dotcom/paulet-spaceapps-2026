The hook

In the 1890s, the Peruvian engineer Pedro Paulet claimed he fired a liquid-fuel rocket engine in Paris, decades before anyone else.

What would it take to finish today what he started?

The gap

Students rarely get to design a whole mission, from the goal to the last day of operations.

The trade-offs

Mass, power, data, budget, orbit and launcher stay abstract when you learn them one at a time.

Our answer

A game where every choice moves at least two resources, and there is never one right design.

The player's experience · 10 minutes

Design. Launch. Fly. Read the letter.

1 · Design

Pick instruments, power, orbit and launcher. A live dashboard shows mass, power, data and budget move with every click.

2 · Launch

A 3D flight deck with real telemetry for the chosen orbit and live cameras: ground, chase, onboard, map.

3 · Fly

Years of operations in minutes. Solar flares, debris and budget cuts: the team decides, with advisors who explain.

4 · Learn why

A score out of 1,000 and a letter to Paulet that explains, in plain words, what worked, what failed and what to change.

The design review never says only "wrong": it says why, and what to change.
